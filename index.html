(RFId001202604272)

https://www.bonares.de/service-portal/data-repository

The BonaRes Repository stores, manage and publishes soil, agricultural and landscape research data from research projects, agricultural long-term field experiments and soil profiles which contribute significantly to the understanding of agriculture and landscape research. This can for instance be changes of soil and soil functions over the long term. Research data are described by the metadata following the BonaRes Metadata Schema (DOI: 10.20387/bonares-5pgg-8yrp) which combines international recognized standards for the description of geospatial data (INSPIRE Directive) and research data (DataCite 4.0). Within the BonaRes Repository research data is provided for free reuse under the CC License and can be discovered by advanced text and map search via a number of criteria.

Edaphobase

(RFId011202604272)

https://portal.edaphobase.org

Edaphobase is a non-commercial data infrastructure developed and hosted by the Senckenberg Museum of Natural History Görlitz in Germany, combining data from heterogeneous sources on soil animals, their distribution and habitat parameters of their sites of occurrence and making this data available to the public in open access. Edaphobase currently includes data on Nematoda, Collembola, Oribatida, Gamasina, Chilopoda, Diplopoda, Isopoda, Enchytraeidae, and Lumbricidae. Edaphobase lives from the cooperation with numerous soil zoologists who upload their data to the database and thus make them available for overarching analyses and insight. The database currently focusses on Europe-wide soil-biodiversity data, but includes data from around the globe. Edaphobase is strongly geared towards common data re-use and provision of data and online analysis tools. It follows the FAIR principles and can offer DOIs for submitted data sets. The data warehouse is publically available (open access) via a web-based browser portal, the Edaphobase data-query portal. All data (provided it is not anonymized or in an embargo period) can be queried and collated via multiple filters and be downloaded by registered users. Simple queries are possible as well as more sophisticated analyses of different data groups. Specific applications include, e.g., mapping taxa’s distributions, the elucidation of species-specific habitat preferences (niche space), or environmental correlations with population densities. Edaphobase is a data provider to the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF).

European Nucleotide Archive

(RFId025202604272)

https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/home

The European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) captures and presents information relating to experimental workflows that are based around nucleotide sequencing. A typical workflow includes the isolation and preparation of material for sequencing, a run of a sequencing machine in which sequencing data are produced and a subsequent bioinformatic analysis pipeline. ENA records this information in a data model that covers input information (sample, experimental setup, machine configuration), output machine data (sequence traces, reads and quality scores) and interpreted information (assembly, mapping, functional annotation). Data arrive at ENA from a variety of sources. These include submissions of raw data, assembled sequences and annotation from small-scale sequencing efforts, data provision from the major European sequencing centres and routine and comprehensive exchange with our partners in the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC). Provision of nucleotide sequence data to ENA or its INSDC partners has become a central and mandatory step in the dissemination of research findings to the scientific community. ENA works with publishers of scientific literature and funding bodies to ensure compliance with these principles and to provide optimal submission systems and data access tools that work seamlessly with the published literature.

German Human Genome-Phenome Archive

(RFId055202604272)

https://data.ghga.de/

The German Human Genome-Phenome Archive (GHGA) is a dedicated repository for storing and sharing diverse genomic and phenotypic data from a wide range of scientific studies, including clinical trials, population research, routine healthcare, and biomedical investigations usually coming from research institutions in Germany. By connecting with international initiatives such as the European Genome-phenome Archive (EGA) and the European Genomic Data Infrastructure (GDI), GHGA ensures that data is FAIRly stored, accessible, and interoperable across the globe. GHGA has set up a trusted, long-term repository, with plans to preserve data at every stage, from raw sequencing files to processed data and research results. Developed in phases, GHGA will continually add features that support secondary research, including secure processing environments, data statistics, and data analysis tools. In this way, GHGA provides a secure and evolving resource for researchers, accelerating scientific discoveries and the development of innovative healthcare solutions. Recognizing the sensitivity of human omics data, GHGA ensures strict data protection through robust security systems that maintain patient privacy and comply with ethical standards. Access to restricted datasets is not managed by GHGA, but by relevant Data Access Committees (DACs) appointed by the respective data controllers. DACs oversee requests based on established ethical and legal guidelines, while complying with the data subject’s consent. GHGA functions as an intermediate - who facilitates access for authorized users, such as researchers and healthcare professionals, ensuring that only those with proper clearance can access controlled data.

The ARChive (archive.nfdi4plants.org) is the central, open-access data publication and long-term storage repository for the German plant science research community.
